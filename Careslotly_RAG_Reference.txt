CareSlotly RAG Reference
========================

Project Overview
----------------
CareSlotly is a doctor-finding and healthcare booking web application built with Flask, SQLAlchemy, Bootstrap, and AI-enabled search. It connects patients with doctors and hospitals, supports patient and doctor portals, and enables appointment booking, consultation scheduling, messaging, and follow-up.

Core Features
-------------
- AI-Powered Doctor Search:
  - Semantic search for symptoms, specialties, and locations.
  - Natural language input support (symptom-based queries like "chest pain" or "skin rash").
  - Autocomplete suggestions for symptoms, specialties, and locations.
  - Location normalization and alias handling for better search coverage.

- Dual User Portals:
  - Patient portal: registration, login, profile, dashboard, appointments, conversations.
  - Doctor portal: registration, login, profile, manage appointments, write prescriptions, messaging.

- Appointment Booking System:
  - Real-time doctor availability and appointment slot filtering.
  - Online and in-person consultation support.
  - Appointment status management: Pending, Confirmed, Completed.
  - View upcoming appointments and history.

- Doctor Profiles and Reviews:
  - Detailed doctor listings with specialization, experience, rating, reviews, hospital affiliation.
  - Patients can submit reviews after consultation.

- Hospital Finder:
  - Search hospitals by location.
  - View hospital services, address, contact, and map link.

- Verification and Security:
  - Email verification using Flask-Mail.
  - Mobile verification via Firebase/Twilio OTP.
  - Password reset with OTP support.

- Chatbot and Health Advice:
  - Chatbot FAQ responses for booking, consultation, and site navigation.
  - Health advice for common conditions and first-response home remedies.
  - Suggested actions through the chatbot: browse doctors, view appointments, contact support.

- Additional Utilities:
  - Search history tracking.
  - Patient dashboard and doctor dashboard metrics.
  - Prescription management by doctors.
  - Profile editing and verification workflows.

Services Quick Answer
---------------------
- CareSlotly helps users find doctors by symptom, specialty, or location.
- Users can book online or in-person appointments.
- Patients can manage appointments, messages, prescriptions, profile, and history.
- Users can find hospitals and emergency guidance.

Website Services Provided
-------------------------
CareSlotly provides the following services:
- Doctor search service: users can search doctors by symptom, disease, specialty, doctor name, hospital, or location.
- AI symptom-to-specialist matching: the system maps symptoms like chest pain, skin rash, tooth pain, headache, fever, back pain, and eye irritation to suitable specialists.
- Browse doctors service: users can view doctors with specialization, hospital, location, ratings, experience, consultation type, and available slots.
- Appointment booking service: patients can book online or in-person appointments by selecting a doctor, consultation type, date, and available time slot.
- Online consultation support: doctors who offer online consultations show online availability, and patients can select online slots when booking.
- In-person consultation support: patients can choose physical visits when the doctor supports in-person appointments.
- Patient portal service: patients can register, login, verify email/mobile, manage profile, view dashboard, see appointments, and track consultation history.
- Doctor portal service: doctors can register, login, manage profile, set availability, view appointment requests, update appointment status, and manage patient communication.
- Messaging service: patient-doctor conversations are available after appointment booking and may remain available during follow-up.
- Prescription service: doctors can write prescriptions for confirmed or completed appointments, and patients can access follow-up information.
- Hospital finder service: users can search hospitals by location and view hospital services, address, contact details, and map links.
- Reviews and ratings service: patients can review doctors after consultation, helping other users compare doctor options.
- Emergency services information: the site provides emergency guidance and quick access to emergency service information.
- AI chatbot assistant: the chatbot helps users find doctors, book appointments, choose specialists, understand website services, get general health guidance, and access emergency guidance.
- RAG/LLM health information service: the assistant can answer general questions about symptoms, causes, remedies, red flags, what doctor to consult, consultation steps, pet and animal care, and helpline guidance.
- Search history and personalization: the system stores recent searches for logged-in patients to make repeat searches and follow-up easier.
- Account verification and security: email verification, mobile OTP support when configured, password reset, and secure login workflows are supported.

How Users Access These Services
-------------------------------
- To find care: search by symptom, disease, specialty, or location, then open matching doctor profiles.
- To book care: choose a doctor, select online or in-person consultation, pick a slot, and submit the appointment request.
- To manage care: login to the patient dashboard to view appointments, profile, messages, history, and follow-ups.
- To use doctor services: doctors login to the doctor portal to manage slots, appointments, patient messages, and prescriptions.
- To find hospitals: open the hospital finder and search by location or service need.
- To get quick help: use the chatbot to ask about services, booking, specialists, symptoms, emergency numbers, or consultation steps.

Common Conditions and Advice
----------------------------
The CareSlotly chatbot contains advice and remedies for the following conditions:

- Back Pain
  - Gentle stretching, posture correction, warm compress.
  - Keep moving with short walks.
  - Consult orthopedics or physiotherapy if severe, persistent, or with numbness.

- Headache
  - Rest in a quiet, dark room.
  - Stay hydrated and avoid bright screens.
  - Seek medical help if sudden, severe, or with vision changes or weakness.

- Migraine
  - Rest in a quiet, dark room.
  - Stay hydrated and avoid strong smells and loud noise.
  - Consult a doctor if pain is severe or accompanied by visual changes.

- Fever
  - Drink fluids, rest, and use a cool compress or paracetamol.
  - See a doctor if fever persists more than two days or has worrying symptoms.

- Cold
  - Rest, drink warm fluids, use saline nasal drops, and humidify the air.
  - Consult a doctor if symptoms last longer than a week or worsen.

- Cough
  - Hydrate, inhale steam, and avoid smoke or dust.
  - Book a doctor if persistent, bloody, or with high fever.

- Sore Throat
  - Drink warm fluids, gargle with salt water, and rest your voice.
  - See a doctor if severe, lasting, or accompanied by fever.

- Stomach Pain
  - Rest, eat light bland foods, avoid spicy or greasy meals.
  - Seek medical attention if severe, persistent, or with vomiting or blood.

- Diarrhea
  - Drink plenty of fluids, use oral rehydration, eat light foods.
  - See a doctor if it continues, contains blood, or causes dehydration.

- Vomiting
  - Sip clear fluids slowly, avoid solid food until it settles.
  - Get care if vomiting is severe, persistent, or fluids cannot be retained.

- Joint Pain
  - Apply ice or heat, rest, and avoid heavy lifting.
  - Consult orthopedist or rheumatologist if sudden swelling or limited movement.

- Allergy
  - Avoid triggers, rinse nose with saline, take antihistamine if needed.
  - Seek urgent help for breathing difficulty, swelling, or spreading rash.

- Acidity
  - Avoid spicy/fatty foods, eat smaller meals, limit caffeine.
  - Seek doctor evaluation if heartburn is frequent or severe.

- Acne
  - Keep skin clean with a gentle cleanser.
  - Avoid squeezing pimples.
  - See a dermatologist if acne persists or is painful.

- Stress
  - Practice deep breathing, take breaks, sleep enough, stay active.
  - Consult a doctor or counselor if stress is overwhelming.

- Fatigue
  - Rest, hydrate, eat balanced meals, and take activity breaks.
  - See a healthcare provider if fatigue continues despite rest.

- Insomnia
  - Keep a regular sleep schedule, avoid screens before bed.
  - Create a calm sleep environment.
  - Consult a provider if poor sleep continues.

- Eye Irritation
  - Rinse eyes with clean water, avoid rubbing, rest from screens.
  - See an eye specialist if irritation persists, causes pain, or affects vision.

Consultation & Booking Workflow
-------------------------------
1. Search:
   - The user enters symptoms, conditions, or locations in the search bar.
   - The system maps symptoms to specialists and nearby locations.
   - Autocomplete assists with symptoms, specialties, and locations.

2. Browse Doctors:
   - Search results list doctors with key details and ratings.
   - Doctors display hospital affiliation, contact, and availability.
   - Filters and suggested specialists help narrow down options.

3. Open Doctor Profile:
   - View detailed profile, reviews, specialties, experience.
   - See available online or in-person slots.
   - Confirm consultation type and patient details.

4. Book Appointment:
   - Select a date/time slot.
   - Choose consultation type: Online or In-Person.
   - Submit booking request.
   - If not logged in, the system prompts for sign-in or registration.

5. Appointment Management:
   - Patients can view upcoming and past appointments.
   - Doctors can confirm, complete, or cancel appointments.
   - Messages can be exchanged between patient and doctor after booking.

6. Follow-Up:
   - Doctors can write prescriptions for completed or confirmed appointments.
   - Patients can review doctors after consultation.
   - Patient dashboard stores search history and appointment records.

Expanded Medical and Veterinary Knowledge Base
==============================================

Safety Scope for RAG/LLM Responses
----------------------------------
- The assistant must provide general education, first-aid orientation, and doctor-selection guidance only.
- The assistant must not claim to diagnose a patient, prescribe medicines, replace a licensed doctor or veterinarian, or delay urgent care.
- For severe, sudden, worsening, unusual, or persistent symptoms, advise urgent evaluation by a qualified clinician.
- For humans in India, call 112 for police, fire, health, rescue, women safety, child protection, threat-to-life, and other emergencies through the Emergency Response Support System. 108 or 102 may also be used for ambulance services where active locally.
- For the United States, call 911 for emergencies and 988 for suicide or mental-health crisis support.
- For poisoning, overdose, snakebite, severe allergic reaction, breathing difficulty, chest pain, stroke signs, major injury, seizures, severe bleeding, unconsciousness, severe dehydration, or suicidal thoughts, seek emergency help immediately.
- For pets, birds, livestock, and wild animals, contact a local veterinarian, veterinary emergency hospital, government animal husbandry department, animal ambulance, or recognized animal rescue service. In some Indian states, 1962 is used for animal ambulance or mobile veterinary support; availability is state-dependent.

General Consultation Steps
--------------------------
1. Identify the main problem: body part, symptom, duration, severity, triggers, injuries, fever, vomiting, bleeding, breathing issues, pain score, and existing diseases.
2. Check for red flags: severe pain, chest pain, shortness of breath, fainting, confusion, weakness on one side, persistent high fever, blood in vomit/stool/urine, severe dehydration, poisoning, bites, burns, pregnancy complications, infant illness, or rapid worsening.
3. Choose the right care level: emergency helpline for danger signs, urgent care for same-day moderate symptoms, routine appointment for stable non-urgent symptoms, teleconsult for mild follow-up questions.
4. Choose the right specialist: use the symptom-to-specialist mapping below and book through CareSlotly by searching symptom, specialty, or location.
5. Prepare for the visit: carry medicines list, allergies, reports, photos of rashes/injuries, vaccination records, pregnancy status, previous diagnoses, and questions.
6. Follow up: complete tests, take medicines exactly as prescribed, return if symptoms worsen, and use CareSlotly appointments or messages for follow-up when available.

Human Body Parts, Common Problems, Causes, Remedies, and Specialists
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Head, Brain, and Nerves
- Common issues: headache, migraine, concussion, dizziness, seizure, stroke symptoms, facial weakness, memory changes, numbness, tingling.
- Possible causes: dehydration, poor sleep, stress, eye strain, sinus infection, migraine disorder, high blood pressure, infection, head injury, nerve compression, stroke, brain inflammation.
- Self-care for mild symptoms: rest in a quiet room, hydrate, avoid alcohol, reduce screen strain, use a cold or warm compress, track triggers, and avoid driving if dizzy.
- Red flags: sudden worst headache, weakness on one side, facial droop, speech trouble, seizure, confusion, fainting, head injury with vomiting, fever with stiff neck.
- Consult: neurologist for seizures, migraine, stroke-like symptoms, numbness, weakness, tremor, memory issues; emergency physician for sudden severe symptoms; general physician for mild recurrent headache.

Eyes
- Common issues: eye irritation, redness, itching, dry eyes, blurred vision, eye pain, injury, discharge, light sensitivity.
- Possible causes: allergy, infection, dry eye, foreign body, screen strain, corneal injury, glaucoma, diabetes-related eye disease.
- Self-care for mild symptoms: rinse with clean water, avoid rubbing, rest from screens, use clean compresses, avoid sharing towels or cosmetics.
- Red flags: eye injury, chemical splash, severe pain, sudden vision loss, halos, unequal pupils, high fever with swelling around eye.
- Consult: ophthalmologist or eye specialist; emergency care for injury, chemical exposure, or sudden vision changes.

Ears, Nose, Throat, and Sinuses
- Common issues: ear pain, hearing loss, ringing, blocked nose, sinus pain, sore throat, tonsillitis, hoarseness, nosebleed.
- Possible causes: viral infection, allergy, ear wax, middle ear infection, sinusitis, acid reflux, voice strain, injury.
- Self-care for mild symptoms: fluids, saline nasal spray, steam or humidified air, salt-water gargle, voice rest, avoid smoke and dust.
- Red flags: breathing difficulty, drooling, severe throat swelling, high fever, stiff neck, blood after injury, sudden hearing loss.
- Consult: ENT specialist; pediatrician for children; emergency care for airway trouble.

Mouth, Teeth, and Jaw
- Common issues: toothache, gum bleeding, mouth ulcers, bad breath, jaw pain, facial swelling.
- Possible causes: cavities, gum disease, dental abscess, injury, wisdom tooth, vitamin deficiency, oral infection, temporomandibular joint strain.
- Self-care for mild symptoms: gentle brushing, flossing, warm salt-water rinse, avoid very hot/cold/sugary foods, do not place aspirin on gums.
- Red flags: facial swelling, fever, difficulty swallowing, spreading infection, trauma, uncontrolled bleeding.
- Consult: dentist, oral surgeon for complex extraction or jaw problems, ENT or physician for persistent mouth ulcers.

Chest, Lungs, and Breathing
- Common issues: cough, wheezing, asthma, bronchitis, pneumonia, chest tightness, shortness of breath.
- Possible causes: viral infection, allergy, asthma, smoking, pollution, pneumonia, tuberculosis, heart disease, pulmonary embolism.
- Self-care for mild symptoms: fluids, rest, avoid smoke/dust, use prescribed inhalers correctly, humidified air.
- Red flags: severe breathing difficulty, blue lips, chest pain, coughing blood, oxygen drop, high fever, confusion, worsening asthma.
- Consult: pulmonologist for asthma, chronic cough, COPD, tuberculosis, breathlessness; emergency physician for acute breathing trouble.

Heart and Blood Vessels
- Common issues: chest pain, palpitations, high blood pressure, swelling in legs, fainting, breathlessness on exertion.
- Possible causes: heart attack, angina, arrhythmia, hypertension, heart failure, anemia, thyroid disease, anxiety, dehydration.
- Self-care for stable known problems: follow prescribed medicines, reduce salt, stop tobacco, exercise as advised, monitor blood pressure, keep follow-up appointments.
- Red flags: chest pressure, pain spreading to arm/jaw/back, sweating, breathlessness, fainting, sudden severe weakness.
- Consult: cardiologist; emergency services for suspected heart attack or dangerous rhythm symptoms.

Stomach, Intestines, and Digestion
- Common issues: acidity, nausea, vomiting, diarrhea, constipation, stomach pain, bloating, food poisoning, piles.
- Possible causes: indigestion, infection, contaminated food, gastritis, ulcers, gallbladder disease, appendicitis, irritable bowel syndrome, inflammatory bowel disease.
- Self-care for mild symptoms: oral rehydration solution, small bland meals, avoid alcohol/spicy/fatty food, rest, hand hygiene.
- Red flags: severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, blood in stool/vomit, black stools, dehydration, rigid belly, pregnancy with abdominal pain.
- Consult: gastroenterologist for recurrent acidity, chronic diarrhea/constipation, liver/gallbladder/pancreas concerns; general physician for mild acute illness; emergency care for red flags.

Liver, Gallbladder, and Pancreas
- Common issues: jaundice, right upper abdominal pain, vomiting, dark urine, pale stool, severe upper abdominal pain.
- Possible causes: hepatitis, alcohol-related liver disease, fatty liver, gallstones, pancreatitis, medication toxicity.
- Self-care: avoid alcohol, avoid unnecessary medicines, hydrate, follow medical diet advice, do not self-medicate for jaundice.
- Red flags: yellow eyes with confusion, severe abdominal pain, persistent vomiting, bleeding, very dark urine, fever with jaundice.
- Consult: gastroenterologist or hepatologist; emergency physician for severe pain or jaundice with confusion.

Kidneys, Urine, and Bladder
- Common issues: burning urination, frequent urination, blood in urine, kidney stones, flank pain, swelling, reduced urine.
- Possible causes: urinary tract infection, dehydration, kidney stone, prostate enlargement, kidney disease, diabetes, high blood pressure.
- Self-care for mild urinary irritation: drink water, do not hold urine, avoid irritants like excess caffeine, seek testing if symptoms persist.
- Red flags: fever with flank pain, pregnancy with UTI symptoms, blood in urine, inability to pass urine, severe pain, reduced urine with swelling.
- Consult: urologist for stones, prostate, urinary obstruction, blood in urine; nephrologist for kidney disease; gynecologist for recurrent UTI in women when appropriate.

Reproductive and Sexual Health
- Common issues: menstrual pain, irregular periods, heavy bleeding, vaginal discharge, pelvic pain, pregnancy symptoms, erectile dysfunction, testicular pain, sexually transmitted infections.
- Possible causes: hormonal changes, pregnancy, infection, endometriosis, fibroids, ovarian cysts, prostate or testicular conditions, diabetes, stress, medication effects.
- Self-care for mild symptoms: track cycles, maintain hygiene, avoid unprotected sex, use barrier protection, avoid douching, hydrate, rest.
- Red flags: pregnancy bleeding, severe pelvic pain, fainting, heavy bleeding soaking pads rapidly, testicular torsion signs, sexual assault, severe infection signs.
- Consult: gynecologist or obstetrician for women/pregnancy; urologist or andrologist for male reproductive concerns; dermatologist/venereologist for STI care.

Skin, Hair, and Nails
- Common issues: rash, acne, allergy, eczema, fungal infection, hair loss, wounds, burns, bites, nail infection.
- Possible causes: allergy, infection, sweat/friction, autoimmune disease, hormones, stress, medication reaction, parasites, nutritional deficiency.
- Self-care for mild symptoms: keep area clean and dry, avoid scratching, use gentle soap, avoid triggers, protect wounds, do not pop boils or severe acne.
- Red flags: rapidly spreading rash, fever, skin peeling, facial/lip swelling, pus with fever, burns, animal bites, blackening skin, painful rash near eye.
- Consult: dermatologist; emergency care for severe allergy, major burns, or spreading infection.

Bones, Joints, Muscles, and Back
- Common issues: joint pain, back pain, neck pain, sprain, fracture, arthritis, swelling, muscle cramps.
- Possible causes: strain, posture, injury, overuse, arthritis, gout, infection, vitamin deficiency, nerve compression.
- Self-care for mild symptoms: rest, gentle movement, ice for new injury, heat for muscle tightness, posture correction, avoid heavy lifting.
- Red flags: deformity, inability to bear weight, numbness/weakness, loss of bladder/bowel control, fever with joint swelling, major trauma.
- Consult: orthopedist for fractures, joint pain, back/neck pain; rheumatologist for autoimmune arthritis; physiotherapist for rehabilitation after medical evaluation.

Hormones, Diabetes, and Metabolism
- Common issues: diabetes, thyroid symptoms, weight change, excessive thirst, frequent urination, fatigue, heat/cold intolerance.
- Possible causes: insulin resistance, autoimmune disease, thyroid disease, adrenal problems, diet/activity imbalance, medication effects.
- Self-care: balanced meals, regular activity, medication adherence, blood sugar monitoring if advised, foot care in diabetes.
- Red flags: confusion, very high or low blood sugar symptoms, vomiting with diabetes, severe dehydration, chest pain, pregnancy with diabetes symptoms.
- Consult: endocrinologist or diabetologist; general physician for screening and initial management.

Blood, Immunity, and Infections
- Common issues: anemia, repeated infections, fever, swollen lymph nodes, unexplained bruising, allergies.
- Possible causes: nutritional deficiency, chronic disease, viral/bacterial infection, autoimmune disease, blood disorders, medication effects.
- Self-care: rest, hydration, nutrition, vaccination as advised, hand hygiene, avoid self-starting antibiotics.
- Red flags: fever with rash/neck stiffness, severe weakness, bleeding, breathlessness, persistent high fever, immunocompromised patient with fever.
- Consult: general physician, infectious disease specialist, hematologist, immunologist/allergist depending on symptoms.

Mental Health, Sleep, and Substance Use
- Common issues: anxiety, depression, stress, panic attacks, insomnia, addiction, suicidal thoughts.
- Possible causes: life stress, trauma, genetics, medical illness, substance use, sleep disruption, medication effects.
- Self-care for mild symptoms: regular sleep, exercise, supportive conversations, breathing exercises, reduce alcohol/drugs, structured routine.
- Red flags: suicidal thoughts, self-harm, hallucinations, severe agitation, confusion, inability to care for self, overdose.
- Consult: psychiatrist, psychologist, counselor, emergency services for crisis; in India, Tele-MANAS mental health support may be available through 14416 or 1800-891-4416, and KIRAN mental health rehabilitation helpline has been listed as 1800-599-0019.

Children and Infants
- Common issues: fever, cough, diarrhea, vomiting, rash, feeding difficulty, ear pain, injury, poor growth.
- Possible causes: viral infections, dehydration, allergies, asthma, ear infection, nutritional issues, injury.
- Self-care for mild symptoms: fluids or breastfeeding, rest, temperature monitoring, oral rehydration for diarrhea, avoid adult medicines unless prescribed.
- Red flags: infant under 3 months with fever, breathing difficulty, blue lips, dehydration, seizures, lethargy, persistent vomiting, severe rash, poor feeding.
- Consult: pediatrician; emergency care for red flags.

Older Adults
- Common issues: falls, confusion, weakness, chest pain, breathlessness, urinary symptoms, memory problems, medication side effects.
- Possible causes: infection, dehydration, stroke, heart disease, fractures, diabetes, low blood pressure, medicine interactions.
- Self-care: medication review, hydration, fall-proofing home, regular checkups, assistive devices.
- Red flags: sudden confusion, fall with injury, chest pain, stroke signs, severe breathlessness, inability to walk.
- Consult: geriatrician, general physician, relevant specialist, emergency care for acute changes.

Emergency Helpline Guidance
---------------------------
- India emergency: 112 for police, fire, health, rescue, women safety, child protection, and threat-to-life emergencies. Use the 112 India app or phone SOS features where available.
- India ambulance: 108 or 102 may be available in many states/UTs; if uncertain, call 112.
- India mental health: Tele-MANAS support may be reachable at 14416 or 1800-891-4416; KIRAN mental health rehabilitation helpline has been listed as 1800-599-0019.
- India child protection: 1098 is widely used for children in distress; if immediate danger, call 112.
- India women in distress: 112 for immediate emergency; 181 or 1091 may be available depending on state/service.
- India cyber financial fraud: 1930 is commonly used for cybercrime reporting; also use the official cybercrime portal.
- United States emergency: 911 for immediate danger; 988 for suicide and crisis lifeline; Poison Control 1-800-222-1222 for poison exposure.
- Always advise users to verify local helplines for their city/state/country because numbers can change or vary.

Pet, Bird, and Animal Health Knowledge Base
-------------------------------------------

General Animal Safety Rules
- Do not give human medicines to animals unless a veterinarian specifically prescribes them.
- Keep sick animals warm, quiet, hydrated when safe, and away from other animals until assessed.
- Bring vaccination records, diet details, recent toxin/food exposure, stool/vomit photos, and symptom timing to the veterinarian.
- Emergency signs in animals include breathing difficulty, collapse, seizures, severe bleeding, inability to urinate, bloated abdomen, repeated vomiting, poisoning, trauma, heatstroke, severe pain, pale gums, blue gums, or unconsciousness.
- For bites or scratches to humans, wash with soap and running water for at least 15 minutes and seek medical care for rabies/tetanus risk.

Dogs
- Common issues: vomiting, diarrhea, parvovirus, ticks/fleas, skin itching, ear infection, cough, limping, heatstroke, poisoning, urinary problems.
- Possible causes: diet change, infection, parasites, toxins, heat exposure, injury, allergies, kidney/liver disease, foreign body obstruction.
- Safe first steps: offer small amounts of water if alert, stop rich foods, keep cool during heat stress, prevent licking wounds, isolate from other dogs if infectious disease is possible.
- Red flags: repeated vomiting, blood in stool, bloated abdomen, pale gums, collapse, seizure, heatstroke, puppy with diarrhea, inability to urinate, suspected poison.
- Consult: veterinarian; emergency veterinary hospital for red flags; veterinary dermatologist for chronic skin disease; veterinary surgeon for fractures/obstruction.

Cats
- Common issues: vomiting, hairballs, urinary blockage, kidney disease, respiratory infection, wounds, abscess, fleas, appetite loss.
- Possible causes: hair ingestion, infection, stress, urinary crystals, kidney disease, toxins, dental disease, foreign body.
- Safe first steps: keep indoors, provide water, do not force-feed a distressed cat, use a carrier for transport, keep litter information for vet.
- Red flags: male cat straining to urinate, not eating for 24 hours, breathing difficulty, severe lethargy, poisoning, repeated vomiting, trauma.
- Consult: veterinarian; emergency vet for urinary blockage or breathing distress.

Birds and Poultry
- Common issues: fluffed feathers, reduced eating, diarrhea, breathing noise, wing injury, egg binding, feather plucking, mites, sudden death in flock.
- Possible causes: infection, poor nutrition, stress, toxins, injury, parasites, egg-laying complications, overheating, poor ventilation.
- Safe first steps: keep bird warm and quiet, avoid fumes/smoke/aerosols, separate sick bird, provide clean water, avoid handling unless necessary.
- Red flags: open-mouth breathing, sitting at cage bottom, bleeding feather, egg binding, seizures, trauma, sudden flock illness.
- Consult: avian veterinarian; livestock/poultry vet or animal husbandry department for farm birds; emergency vet for breathing distress or egg binding.

Rabbits and Small Mammals
- Common issues: not eating, dental overgrowth, diarrhea, bloating, heat stress, skin mites, injury.
- Possible causes: dental disease, low-fiber diet, gut stasis, infection, parasites, heat exposure.
- Safe first steps: keep quiet and cool, provide hay and water, avoid sudden diet changes, do not delay care if not eating.
- Red flags: not eating or no stool, bloated abdomen, severe diarrhea, heatstroke, breathing difficulty.
- Consult: exotic pet veterinarian or veterinarian experienced with rabbits/small mammals.

Cattle, Buffalo, Goats, Sheep, Horses, and Livestock
- Common issues: fever, diarrhea, bloat, mastitis, lameness, wounds, birthing difficulty, reduced milk, respiratory disease, parasites.
- Possible causes: infection, feed change, contaminated water, injury, poor hoof care, reproductive complications, parasites.
- Safe first steps: isolate sick animal, provide shade/water, avoid forcing feed, record temperature if trained, call local livestock veterinarian.
- Red flags: bloat, inability to stand, difficult birth, severe bleeding, snakebite, suspected infectious outbreak, sudden death, severe dehydration.
- Consult: livestock veterinarian, government veterinary hospital, animal husbandry officer, emergency animal ambulance where available.

Wild Animals and Strays
- Do not handle wild animals, bats, monkeys, snakes, or aggressive strays without trained rescue support.
- For injured wildlife, contact local forest department, wildlife rescue, animal welfare organization, or police/112 if public safety is at risk.
- For snakebite in humans or animals, treat as emergency. Keep the patient still, avoid cutting/sucking the wound, and seek urgent care.
- For rabies risk from dog/cat/monkey/bat bites, seek immediate medical care for wound cleaning, rabies vaccination guidance, and tetanus protection.

Animal Disease-to-Doctor Mapping
--------------------------------
- Pet vomiting, diarrhea, appetite loss: veterinarian.
- Pet skin disease, itching, hair loss: veterinarian; veterinary dermatologist if chronic.
- Pet fracture, wound, swallowed object: emergency vet or veterinary surgeon.
- Bird breathing problem, egg binding, feather disease: avian veterinarian.
- Livestock fever, bloat, mastitis, calving/kidding difficulty: livestock veterinarian or animal husbandry department.
- Human bitten/scratched by animal: human doctor, emergency department, or infectious disease/rabies clinic; animal also needs veterinary assessment.
